D.O.112, s.2024 · classification and conversion guide

Road class follows network function under Section I, rather than ADT or geometric width. Existing inventory/approved classification must be verified. Primary/Secondary/Tertiary here are distinct from the Arterial/Collector/Local geometric-design controls.

Conversion requirements: an existing road passable to a four-wheeled vehicle; field validation of 6.10 m carriageway, at least gravel surfacing and maintainable condition; proof of acquired minimum 20 m RROW; proposal/technical documents and official evaluation. Bypass/diversion SRCs must be paved and satisfy Attachment 2. These requirements do not replace DGCS design criteria.

Incomplete documents leave the SRC in its current local/unclassified status. This worksheet neither approves conversion nor changes the official inventory.

Pavement and shoulder widths follow the schedule's project-adopted values, otherwise its baseline. The example's 3.05 m lanes and 2.5–3.0 m shoulders do not override selected criteria. Crossfall 2% and shoulder fall 3% are initial illustration values; select Normal crown or Superelevated curve. Superelevation rotates about the finished centreline; shoulders continue the same plane. The displayed rate is checked against the schedule’s maximum, but curve radius, side friction, shoulder rollover and transition/runoff lengths require separate design.

Ground levels are measured at the outer shoulder edges, relative to finished centreline. Initial +1 / −1 m values demonstrate mixed cut/fill only; replace them with survey values. Ground between and beyond those points is modeled as a straight line. Areas are measured to the underside of all entered layers. Blank base/subbase thicknesses are TO BE ESTABLISHED and omitted from geometry; the resulting earthwork areas are provisional. Lane width defaults to half the selected two-lane baseline; lane count expands the illustrated section and does not establish capacity. A median is a reserved flush strip, not a designed raised/structural median. Drainage and barriers are schematic display symbols; their dimensions, placement and earthworks require design. The drawing applies the entered layer depths across the platform; confirm the project shoulder construction separately. ROW, berms and drainage geometry need project dimensions.

Tourism Table 3-2: PCC 230 mm / 1.5% cross slope; ACP 50 mm / 2%; shoulder ≥1.5 m with gravel surfacing. These provisions are tourism-specific. Table 7-1 assumes berms and slope protection; granular fill assumes adequate foundation capacity and drained, unsaturated conditions. Rock cut slopes require §7.3 assessment. Scope & Review Notes

Adopted values are retained when controls change. Reset clears them. For 95 km/h, Table 3-8 has no exact row: no stopping-distance interpolation is assumed. Table 3-1 non-passing distance and Table 3-8 stopping distance remain separate. Radius, grade and older passing-distance baselines are not recalculated for a changed speed.

Expressways need a divided, multilane section; the two-lane illustration is disabled. §3.1 requires at least two 3.6 m lanes in each direction; §3.4 specifies ≥80 km/h, even though Table 3-1 includes lower speeds. No contradictory provision is silently discarded. Tourism surfacing thickness and cross slopes are not applied to other road classes. Functional urban or unlisted cases require project review. National-road grade compensation and approval requirements are in §3.6.3.1 (p. 3-39).
