Alignment Curve Checker

Build a local alignment from stations, radius, angle and turn direction. Results and review items update as you edit.

PI stations locate the curve along the alignment. Radius, intersection angle and L/R turn define its shape. No survey coordinates are required.

Input schedule

Elements of curve

Enter PI station, angle, radius and Left/Right for each curve. Angles accept decimal degrees or 68°35′12″; stations accept 2+100 or 2100. PC = PI − T; PT = PC + Lc. In spacing mode, enter the first PC station and the tangent from the previous PT to the next PC.

Rs is the radius of the vehicle path at the center of the inside lane, not automatically the alignment radius. S is the adopted required sight distance along that path. Available offset is the minimum clear inward distance from that lane path to an obstruction. All values are metres; verify the obstacle/eye-height envelope separately. This single-curve check requires S ≤ Rs × Δ.

Project screening criteria & assumptions

Defaults come from the supplied brief. They are project screening settings, not verified universal DPWH limits. Same-direction curves with a positive tangent shorter than the screen receive a broken-back warning. A longer tangent does not establish full acceptance. Tolerance: 0.05 m for station/table/positional checks; 0.01° for tangent headings. Gaps within ±0.05 m are treated as direct connections for screening.

Horizontal sight-offset method and limits

HSO = Rs[1 − cos(S/(2Rs))], with the angle in radians. The comparison uses available offset ≥ required HSO without a 0.05 m acceptance allowance. Both sightline endpoints must lie on the same simple circular curve; required S must not exceed the lane-path arc length Rs × Δ (Δ in radians). This is an idealized constant-clearance horizontal envelope check, not a 3D visibility analysis, a computation of speed-based stopping sight distance, or a check across reverse/compound transitions.

Geometry and scope reference: TxDOT Roadway Design Manual §4.7.9. The referenced geometry is unit-consistent in metres; no TxDOT design-speed limits are adopted as DPWH criteria.

Method, references & use

Simple circular curves only: Lc = πRΔ/180; T = R tan(Δ/2); long chord = 2R sin(Δ/2); external = R[sec(Δ/2) − 1]; middle ordinate = R[1 − cos(Δ/2)]. A right turn increases azimuth; a left turn decreases it. The plot integrates the circular geometry and retains equal coordinate scales.

Station mode reconstructs successive circular curves in a local frame. PI tangent station controls PC = PI − T and the intervening tangent to the preceding PT. Alternatively, first PC station and tangent spacings control the chain. Starting origin and orientation are arbitrary; the result is not a georeferenced plan.

Invalid or incomplete geometry clears the results and hides the checklist. Overlaps remain plotted as deficiencies and prevent a continuous-alignment interpretation. The checklist recomputes its Yes / No / N/A / Not evaluated findings from the current result.

Geometry reference: FHWA Bridge Geometry Manual, HIF-22-034, §2.5–2.6. Project-specific context: DPWH published project plan general notes. Access to the DPWH PDF content was limited; this application uses the user-supplied 30 m and 1.5 screening values, without claiming comprehensive DGCS compliance.
