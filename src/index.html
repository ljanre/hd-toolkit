Rigid Pavement Design · Calculation Steps & Nomographs

  1. Establish Design Traffic · W18

    Use cumulative 18-kip ESALs in the design lane over the design period. The Design Traffic tab uses Ni = 365 × AADTopening,i × Gi × DD × DL, then W18 = Σ(Ni × EFi). Transfer a complete result or enter the adopted design traffic in millions.

    W18 = Design Traffic (million) × 1,000,000 Eq. (1)

    Two lanes total means one per direction: assumed DD = 0.50 and DL = 1.00. AADT, growth and axle factors remain project inputs.

  2. Determine Reliability and Variability

    Look up or interpolate ZR from the active R–ZR table. So represents the adopted overall standard deviation for rigid pavement.

    Reliability Term = ZR × So Eq. (2)

  3. Calculate Serviceability Loss

    ΔPSI = p₀ − pₜ Eq. (3)

    Initial serviceability must exceed terminal. Both are serviceability indices, not pressure units.

  4. Calculate Concrete Properties

    Sc(psi) = 550 (standard toggle) or entered project specification Eq. (4)

    SDs = (PS/100) × Sc; S′c = Sc + |ZR| × SDs Eq. (5)

    Ec(psi) = 57,000√[f′c(MPa) × 145.038] Eq. (6)

  1. Determine Composite Subgrade Reaction · Figure 3.3

    Enter subbase thickness DSB, subbase elastic modulus ESB and roadbed resilient modulus MR. The digital nomograph follows the plotted scales without extrapolation.

    Composite k∞: — pci

    Figure 3.3 graphical model allowance: ±6%; this is not a statistical confidence interval.

  2. Determine Effective k

    Use the composite result only when seasonal and shallow-foundation adjustments do not change k. Otherwise select Adjusted Effective k and enter the value after those project-specific adjustments.

    Effective k before loss of support: — pci

  3. Correct for Loss of Support · Figure 3.6

    From effective k on the horizontal axis, move upward to the selected LS line, then left to read kcorr. LS = 0 leaves k unchanged; fractional LS interpolates log10(kcorr) between adjacent printed lines.

    Corrected kcorr: —

    Printed correction range: effective k 1–2,000 pci, LS 0–3, corrected k 1–1,000 pci. The result is an approximate chart reading, rounded to whole pci for slab design.

  1. Adopt Drainage and Load Transfer Coefficients

    Choose Cd for drainage quality and time near saturation; choose J for joints, load-transfer devices and shoulder conditions. The input guides contain the reference tables.

  2. Solve the AASHTO 1993 Rigid Slab Equation

    log10(W18) = ZR × So + 7.35 log10(D + 1) − 0.06
  + log10(ΔPSI / 3) / [1 + 1.624 × 10⁷ / (D + 1)^8.46]
  + (4.22 − 0.32pt) × log10{S′c × Cd × (D^0.75 − 1.132)
    / [215.63 × J × (D^0.75 − 18.42 / (Ec / kcorr)^0.25)]}    Eq. (7)

    D is in inches; S′c and Ec are in psi; kcorr is in pci. The solver brackets the rising-capacity branch, then solves until the equation residual converges. Invalid logarithm domains are rejected.

  3. Round the Thickness and Check Capacity

    D(mm) = 25.4 × D(in); Drounded = ceiling(D / increment) × increment Eq. (8)

    Rounding is a numerical option. Adopt construction thickness using the applicable project criteria. Foundation Support Subbase Thickness controls both the drawing and automatic kcorr. With auto-calculate off, the entered kcorr is independent of drawing thickness.
